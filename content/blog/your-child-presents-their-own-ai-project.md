The first time your child shows you something they built with AI, you remember it.

Not because the project is perfect. Because the voice is different. They are not asking a question. They are explaining.

That moment is what this programme is built around.

## Here is the shape of it

Six weeks. One session per week. Each session runs ninety minutes. Groups are capped at fourteen students. Everything happens in person, in Birmingham, taught by Aston University MSc specialists.

By the sixth session, every child has a finished AI project they built themselves, and the confidence to walk you through it from start to finish.

## What your child leaves with

- A completed AI project of their own, with the confidence to explain how it works.
- Practical prompting and AI-tool skills they can use on their homework the next day.
- Critical AI literacy: knowing when to trust an answer, and when not to.
- A head start on the exact skills universities and employers now scan for.

## Why we built this

Schools are still catching up. Most children are already using AI. Very few are being taught how to use it well.

There is a difference between a child who types a question into a chatbot and a child who can design a prompt, judge the answer, and build something useful with it. The gap is not about intelligence. It is about exposure. We close it in six weeks.

## Who it is for, and what it costs

Two tracks, both run as full six-week blocks.

- **KS3 (Years 7 to 9).** Meeting AI, making things, understanding the world they are growing up into. **£180** for the full block.
- **KS4/5 (Years 10 to 13).** Building, deploying, and thinking critically about AI's role in study and work. **£240** for the full block.

Blocks are block-only. Pay once. Your child sits all six sessions. They leave the last session with their own project in hand.

## Why the group is capped at fourteen

Fourteen is small enough that every child speaks in every session. It is small enough that the final showcase lands as personal, not performative. It is also the reason seats fill fast. When a block is full, it is full.

## What the final evening looks like

Six weeks from the day your child starts, you walk into a room and watch them present. Not a worksheet. Not a quiz result. A thing they built. They explain what it does, what they tried that did not work, and what they would do next.

That evening is the point of the whole programme.
